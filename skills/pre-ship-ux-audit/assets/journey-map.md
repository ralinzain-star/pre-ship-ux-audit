# Journey map: [feature name]

The artefact you put on a wall. `flow-template.md` is the machine's view, steps and branches
and states. This is the person's view: what they are trying to get done, what the product
asks of them, and where it costs them something.

Build it **after** the flow and the JTBD, from both. It is a reading of those, not a third
opinion.

One row per phase, three to six phases. If you have ten, they are steps, not phases.

---

## Who this is

One sentence. Not a persona card: the situation they are in when they arrive.

> A marketing manager who decided last night to move into product, with a resume that says
> marketing and two hours after the kids are asleep.

## The map

| | Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Phase 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What they are trying to get done** | The job, in their words | | |
| **What they do** | The two or three actions that matter | | |
| **What the product asks of them** | Input, a decision, a wait, a payment | | |
| **What they are thinking** | From evidence, not imagination | | |
| **Where it costs them** | The friction, named | | |
| **Coverage** | `agreed` / `specified-unbuilt` / `absent` | | |

The last row is what makes this more than a poster. A phase whose row is `absent` is one
nobody has designed, and it will read as a hole in the wall, which is the point.

## Where it breaks

Three to five moments, no more. Each one: the phase, what happens, and what it costs the
user. Pull them from the audit's Criticals rather than writing new ones, so the map and the
scorecard cannot drift apart.

## Where the product is good

Two or three. A map with no highlights gets read as an attack and discounted, and the team
stops telling you things. If something genuinely works, the map is where the team sees it
next to the rest of the journey rather than buried in a pass row.

## What we do not know

The questions a person could answer and the build cannot. Keep them as questions, do not
resolve them with a guess, and mark any that block a fix.

## Rules

**Evidence, not empathy.** "What they are thinking" comes from a task run, a support ticket,
a session recording or a quote. If you have none, write the row as a question instead of
inventing a feeling. An invented emotion row is how a journey map loses a team's trust.

**Three to six phases.** Phases are chapters of intent, not screens. If a phase has one
screen it is a step; if it has fifteen it is two phases.

**No phase without a coverage value.** The point of building this after the flow is that you
already know which parts exist.

**It is a reading, not a source.** Every claim traces to the flow, the JTBD or a finding.
When the audit is re-run and something changes, this changes with it.
